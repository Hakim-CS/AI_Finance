

However, three issues prevent a higher score:
First, the Mechatronics justification is the thesis's weakest component. 
Chapter 3 reads as a defense brief, not a natural framing. A Mechatronics professor
 must ask: could this work have been done differently if the student had been thinking 
 in Mechatronics terms from day one? Almost certainly yes — a real spending sensor 
 (receipt scanner with embedded hardware, wearable payment tracker), a real-time control 
 loop operating at transaction level rather than monthly, or even a simulation with a proper physical plant 
 analogy would have grounded the framing convincingly. As submitted, the Mechatronics framing is cosmetic.

Second, the dataset size makes all ML metrics suspect. 115 training rows for eight models is not machine 
learning — it is curve fitting. The high R² values should be treated with deep skepticism.

Third, and most importantly, the system is never tested on real data. A thesis that builds a personal finance system 
and never uses it with a real person — even a single pilot user — misses its most natural validation opportunity.
